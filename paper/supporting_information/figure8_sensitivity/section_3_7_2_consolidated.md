### 3.7.2. Pareto efficiency and ranking sensitivity

Pareto efficiency depended on the chemical objective. The S1 frontier contained six generated sets: MatterGen Base, MatterGen Fine-tuned, Con-CDVAE Base, Con-CDVAE Fine-tuned, CrystalFormer Fine-tuned, and Crys-JEPA Base. S2 retained four efficient sets: MatterGen Base, Con-CDVAE Base, Crys-JEPA Base, and Crys-JEPA Refined. S3 reduced the frontier to MatterGen Base and Con-CDVAE Base. These memberships identify alternatives that cannot improve one criterion without sacrificing another within the evaluated population. Figure 7B displays S1 only; scenario-specific memberships and criterion values for S2 and S3 are reported in Table S8.

Weight sensitivity further distinguished these alternatives. Across 20000 weight combinations sampled from Dirichlet(1,1,1), with seed 1984, first-place frequencies in S1 were 41.825% for Con-CDVAE Base, 32.515% for MatterGen Base, and 25.660% for MatterGen Fine-tuned. In S2, Con-CDVAE Base, Crys-JEPA Base, and Crys-JEPA Refined led in 40.825%, 31.340%, and 25.325% of combinations, respectively; MatterGen Base accounted for the remaining 2.510%. S3 leadership was divided between Con-CDVAE Base (57.025%) and MatterGen Base (42.975%). Figure 7C shows the S1 rank distribution, whereas Figure 7D shows S1 Top-5 frequencies rather than first-place frequencies; S2 and S3 frequencies are provided in Table S8.

Alternative criteria and ablation exposed additional methodological dependence (Table S9). Replacing the original three-objective system A—QC retention, unique-formula yield, and 3d-containing yield—with system C—QC retention, unique-formula yield, and both 3d and lanthanide atomic-content yields—expanded the Pareto frontier from five to seven sets. Crys-JEPA Refined moved from seventh to fourth, with scores of 0.4378 and 0.5799 under the respective normalizations. This comparison concerns A versus C, not S1 versus C. Across all ten sets, QC retention and unique-formula yield were strongly correlated (Pearson r = 0.845; Spearman rho = 0.915). Removing the separate QC objective preserved all three scenario frontiers but reversed the S3 equal-weight leader: Con-CDVAE Base initially exceeded MatterGen Base (0.8874 versus 0.8780), whereas MatterGen Base led after ablation (0.8756 versus 0.8312). The remaining yields retained their values and denominator of 2000; QC filtering and its contribution to these yields therefore persisted.

These results support conditional model selection based on the discovery objective and chosen weights. Sampled ranking frequencies quantify sensitivity within the specified criteria and weight distribution, rather than probabilities of intrinsic superiority. Scores from different criterion systems do not measure directly comparable performance gains. Finally, elemental presence and atomic-content yields are compositional proxies and do not establish magnetic ordering, moments, or functional performance.
