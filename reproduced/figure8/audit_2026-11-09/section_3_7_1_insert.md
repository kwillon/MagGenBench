The 3d-focused scenario (S1) prioritized the production of structurally accepted, compositionally distinct candidates with increased representation of 3d transition metal elements. In the saved scenario calculations, under equal weighting of the three min–max-normalized objectives, MatterGen Base achieved the highest aggregate score (0.7679), followed by Con-CDVAE Base (0.6995) and Crys-JEPA Base (0.6800); their 3d atomic-content yields were 38.98%, 7.50%, and 10.81%, respectively. Con-CDVAE Base and Crys-JEPA Base combined higher QC retention (93.20% and 88.20%) and unique-formula yield (92.10% and 86.50%) than MatterGen Base (82.85% and 69.60%), but had lower 3d atomic-content yields. MatterGen Fine-tuned exhibited a higher 3d atomic-content yield than its baseline counterpart (57.18% versus 38.98%) but lower QC retention (68.80% versus 82.85%) and a substantially lower unique-formula yield (20.60% versus 69.60%), ranking fourth with an aggregate score of 0.6442 and illustrating the trade-off between chemical specialization and compositional exploration.

The lanthanide-focused scenario (S2) produced a different ranking. Crys-JEPA Base ranked first under equal weighting (aggregate score 0.8645), followed by Con-CDVAE Base (0.8392) and MatterGen Base (0.8245), with lanthanide atomic-content yields of 7.37%, 5.36%, and 8.69%, respectively. Crys-JEPA Base therefore led despite a lower target chemical yield than MatterGen Base, supported by its higher QC retention (88.20% versus 82.85%) and unique-formula yield (86.50% versus 69.60%); Con-CDVAE Base retained the highest values for these structural objectives (93.20% and 92.10%). Crys-JEPA Refined also became more competitive, ranking fourth (0.7597) and attaining the highest lanthanide atomic-content yield (10.35%), although its QC retention and unique-formula yield decreased to 77.15% and 43.35%, compared with 88.20% and 86.50% for Crys-JEPA Base. MatterGen Fine-tuned declined to eighth place (0.3109), with a lanthanide atomic-content yield of only 0.0045%. This difference was associated with the contrasting lanthanide representation of the generated populations. In particular, magnetic-domain fine-tuning of MatterGen, Con-CDVAE, CrystalFormer, and CrystaLLM substantially reduced the occurrence of lanthanide-containing compositions, as independently observed in Section 3.3.

The combined 3d–lanthanide coverage scenario (S3) favored Con-CDVAE Base under equal weighting (aggregate score 0.8874), followed by MatterGen Base (0.8780) and Crys-JEPA Base (0.8292), with coverage yields of 53.25%, 77.90%, and 49.15%, respectively. Although MatterGen Base achieved the highest coverage yield, Con-CDVAE Base ranked first because its higher QC retention (93.20% versus 82.85%) and unique-formula yield (92.10% versus 69.60%) outweighed its lower chemical coverage under equal weighting. Crys-JEPA Base combined QC retention of 88.20% and unique-formula yield of 86.50% with lower coverage than either of the two leading sets. Unlike the atomic-content objectives in S1 and S2, this scenario measured whether accepted structures contained at least one element from either configured group. Therefore, it characterized broad coverage of the selected elemental groups rather than their relative abundance within individual compositions; all reported yields were expressed relative to the nominal generation budget of 2000 structures per generated set.
